## Summary of results
We observed substantial reduction in both hospitalization and death against scenario B.
There were approximately 5-10% reduction in hospitalization (using sceanrio B as the reference group), and approximately 5%-15% reduction of death. In general, deaths were more challenging to fit and calibrate.
We also observed summer peaks in many states as well as the entire US, although not all states demonstrated the summer wave.

## Explanation of observed dynamics given model assumptions
Early vaccination combined with broader coverage provided the most reduction in both hospitalization and death.

### How are projections generated for the retrospective period (June 2025-May 2026)?
The retrospective period is generated as model-based counterfactual projection, not by copying observed values. The Fourier seasonal backbone is calibrated using all available hospitalization and death observations up to the fitting cutoff, then used to generate weekly trajectories beginning with the R20 scenario start date. During the first year, the trajectories are interpreted as retrospective counterfactuals under Scenarios A-E, while the second year is the prospective projection period. This matches the R20 instruction that the first year should be treated as a retrospective counterfactual under the specified scenario assumptions.

Practically, Scenario B is the calibrated baseline trajectory. Scenarios A, C, D, and E are generated by applying paired scenario-specific vaccine-effect multipliers to the same underlying stochastic trajectories, preserving comparability across horizons, targets, and scenarios.

### Describe the process used to set or calibrate disease severity, ie P(hosp given current infection) and P(death given current infection) details. What are the datasets used for calibration of the death targets?
We did not explicitly estimate infection-to-hospitalization or infection-to-death probabilities, because the submitted targets are weekly incident hospitalizations and weekly incident deaths, not infections or cases. Disease severity is therefore handled implicitly through target-specific calibration to observed hospitalization and death time series.

For hospitalization, the model directly fits weekly incident hospitalization counts using the NHSN/HRD hospitalization target data. The vaccine effect is applied as protection against hospitalization, consistent with the R20 guidance that VE against hospitalization includes both protection against infection and protection against hospitalization given breakthrough infection.

For deaths, the model fits a separate weekly incident death trajectory using NCHS mortality surveillance data. Death scenario effects are then linked to the hospitalization scenario effects through a short lagged multiplier, rather than through an explicitly estimated P(death∣infection). The death target is calibrated to NCHS weekly incidence deaths by date of death, not report date, as specified in the R20 instructions.

### Describe seasonality implementation, e.g., whether seasonality varies by geography, what is the function used to model seasonal forcing, and which datasets are used to fit seasonal parameters
A composite seasonality force was imposed into the Fourier series: annually, biannually, and monthly. The seasonality varied by state.
